# The Yard Wars charter: don't be evil

Yard Wars is a game for children from 6 years on and for the grown-ups who play with
them. It is made for fun, not for money. Every design decision - a feature, a reward, a
sound, a word on the screen - is checked against this charter before it goes in. If
something fails a check, it doesn't go in, however good it would be for "engagement".

**Fun and motivating, but never addictive.** Players should enjoy every round, feel
proud of getting better, and look forward to what they can unlock and try next. That
motivation comes from the game itself - good fights, new rowdies, goals you set for
yourself - never from pressure, fear of missing out or tricks that keep people hooked.

## 1. No addiction mechanisms

The game should be fun while you play it and easy to put down. It never pulls players
back or keeps them playing longer than they want. Goals and rewards are welcome - as
long as they motivate, wait patiently, and never punish taking a break.

- No reward for coming back: no daily login rewards, no streaks, no "first win of the
  day" bonuses, nothing that is lost when you skip a day.
- Nothing runs out: no time-limited offers, events or rewards, no seasons or challenges
  that expire, no countdowns to "miss out".
- No gambling: no random rewards (loot boxes, gacha, spins, mystery prizes). What a
  reward contains is always shown before you earn it.
- No nagging: no push notifications, no messages outside the game, no pulsing or flashing
  calls to come back. Notices inside the game stay calm.
- No artificial grind: unlocking things must be reachable by playing normally; numbers
  are not tuned to stretch play time.
- Natural stopping points: rounds are short and end clearly; nothing starts the next
  round by itself.

## 2. No graphical violence (for 6 years and up)

Fights are cartoon tag games, not killing.

- No blood, gore, wounds, bodies or death animations. A rowdy who is out goes "poof"
  (stars, smoke, a funny sound) and pops back in.
- No realistic weapons. Weapons look like toys, gadgets or cartoon props (water spray,
  cartoon bombs, blasters), not like real firearms.
- No killing language on the screen: players are "knocked out" or "tagged", not "killed";
  scores count knockouts, not kills or deaths.
- No cruelty, humiliation or taunting of the defeated; no scary or disturbing imagery.
- Sounds and screen shake stay playful, never harsh or frightening.

## 3. Not the least interest in making money

- Free, completely: no price, no in-app purchases, no premium currency, no paid pass,
  no "supporter" items, no paid skips or boosts.
- No ads of any kind, no sponsored content, no affiliate links.
- No data business: no accounts, no tracking, no analytics, no selling or sharing of
  data. Everything stays on the device (see the privacy policy).
- No manipulation towards spending money anywhere else (e.g. donations pushed in the
  game). The source code is open.

## 4. Respect for children

- No chat or free text between players, no public names or profiles; LAN play stays in
  the local network.
- Plain, honest texts: no dark patterns, no tricky buttons, no pressure.
- Everything can be reached without reading much (icons, colours) and without fast
  reflexes being the only way to win (modes against bots, co-op).

## How to use it

For every change, answer these questions (in the commit or the review):

1. Is it fun and motivating for its own sake? Or does it make anyone play longer or come
   back more often than they would on their own?
2. Is there anything random, expiring, or lost by not playing?
3. Could a 6-year-old find anything in it scary, gory or cruel? Does it show or name
   killing, blood or real weapons?
4. Does it cost money, show ads, or send any data anywhere?
5. Would we be happy to explain it to a parent sitting next to the child?

If any answer is the wrong one, change the design. When in doubt, leave it out.
